## 1. WHAT IS BEING BUILT

A repeatable formula for writing a single cold outbound message that can do the
whole job on its own. Presenter claims the framework generated over $15 million.
The deliverable is a four-part email structure plus a set of tests and rules for
each part, and a way of thinking about conversion as a ratio.

## 2. STACK AND PREREQUISITES

- Cold reading (psychological technique, borrowed from mentalists/psychics)

- Stated preference vs revealed preference

## 3. COMMANDS AND CONFIG, VERBATIM

Not a software tutorial — the verbatim artifacts are copy, quoted below.

The worked example email, built across the four steps:

Personalization:
"hey Nick, love your channel man. Very no BS and has helped me get started in
management consulting. Think I can help you with something and maybe return a
bit of the favor you've unwittingly done me."

Identity / who am I:
"I currently work with a 10 mil sub YouTuber, Mr. X, to help him build landing
pages. We have made 3 mil in the last month alone."

Offer:
"I went through your landing page on Maker School and frankly you are bleeding
money, my friend. I'm so confident that even a couple minor changes here could
fix this that I bet I could generate at least 100k for you in the next 60 days.
I do this 100% up front, no strings, would take 5 minutes of your time and only
if I hit 100k would I ask you for a small cut, maybe 15 to 20%."

CTA:
"would you be open to a 15-minute chat? If so, I can give you a ring at 3:30
p.m. today or before 12:00 p.m. tomorrow."

Identity template, stated as a fill-in:
"I currently work with [client — by name, or 'an [industry] client in
[location]'] to help them do [thing], and [thing] is similar to [what they've
done]. We've done [specific number] in the last [time period]."

Offer template, stated as a fill-in:
"I will do X thing in Y time or Z risk mitigation."
Worked variants given:
- "I will generate you $10,000 in 60 days or I'll keep working for free until I do."
- "I will get you 20 calls in 90 days or I'll give you all of your money back."
- "I will do X amazing thing in Y short amount of time or I'll send you a $500
  gift card to your favorite steak place."

Conversion model, stated as a way of thinking rather than arithmetic to perform:
conversion rate ∝ (perceived ROI × trust that you can deliver it) / friction to get started.

## 4. THE BUILD ORDER

Layer A — three components of a successful piece of outbound (18:33–34:54):
1. Goal. Pick ONE action per message. Buckets named: reply, watch/click an asset,
   book a call, buy. Presenter says he uses the first three and rarely goes to buy,
   and does not put pricing in the email. Rule stated: if you cannot describe the
   goal in one sentence, you are not ready to write the campaign. Every message
   should function as a self-contained campaign — strip away every other message
   and this one should still do the whole job.
2. Frame. P2P, "player to player" / person to person, not company to prospect.
   Two tests given: the text message test (would a friend seeing you type this
   think it was personal or a mass email?) and the read-it-back test (would I
   think this was a broadcast or written for me?). Kill corporate signals:
   no "hope this finds you well", no elaborate signature block. Use "I", not "we".
   Short, casual, slightly imperfect. Presenter names deliberately inserting minor
   imperfections and "sent from my iPhone" as a current tactic.
3. Iteration. Treat it as a science experiment. Send 500–1000 for statistical
   backing. Measure a funnel: opens, replies, opt-ins, calls, proposals, closed,
   and lifetime value tied back to the campaign. Cut bottom performers fast,
   write new variants off the top performer, repeat. Data over gut — presenter
   says campaigns he expected to fail have hit ~15% reply rate. Reply-rate arc
   given as illustration: 3.5% → 4.5% → 5% → 8% → 10%. One-shotting a campaign
   is rare.

Layer B — the four-step copywriting formula (34:52 onward):
1. PERSONALIZATION. Highest-ROI real estate in the email because it is the only
   part everyone reads; drop-off is severe (presenter gestures at ~100% at the
   first word to ~50% by the fifth). Composed of a greeting, then an observation
   or thing-in-common, then a segue into the pitch. Must NOT signal that you are
   selling. Goal is to make the reader think "wait, who is this? do I know them?"
   which buys roughly 30 seconds of attention. Two sentences max, one ideal —
   longer reads as less real. Built on cold reading: statements that feel specific
   but apply to ~80% of the population (e.g. "love your channel, very no BS" —
   every creator believes their channel is no-BS). Explicitly warns against LLM
   slop openers, giving "Hey Stacy, love how passionate you are about process
   optimization and aligning corporations with diversity outcomes at Beaver Corp"
   as the anti-pattern. AI scraping a real datum (e.g. college) woven into a
   cold-read template is the optional step up, not the requirement. Also carries
   voluntary disclosure of information — give away a small personal fact to earn trust.
2. WHO AM I / IDENTITY. Answers the reader's second question after "is this a
   scammer?", namely "who are they and why does it matter?" One or two sentences.
   Social proof IS the introduction. Borrow credibility from a named or described
   client. Simultaneously signals in-group membership with the prospect.
3. OFFER. An observation about their specific situation (also cold-readable —
   "you're leaking money on your landing page" applies almost universally), then a
   too-good-to-be-true offer with built-in risk reversal. Prospect risks nothing;
   all risk sits with you. Must be quantified, hyper-specific, and must NOT use a
   range — "20k in 90 days", never "10 to 20k". Reasoning given: a guarantee is
   what cuts through cold-audience skepticism and earns the call, where the offer
   can then be grounded. Offers are relative to the prospect's size — 100k against
   a business doing 600–700k in the window is a ~16% lift; 100k against a $5M
   YouTuber is ~2%. Presenter states he does not actually recommend
   financially-based offers, deferring the reasoning to later in the course.
4. CTA. One specific ask with a specific time. No vague "would you be
   interested?" or "let me know your thoughts". Give concrete slots so the
   prospect takes one step, not a scheduling negotiation. Minimizing steps is the
   governing principle — every extra step leaks prospects.

## 5. GOTCHAS

- Over-personalizing backfires: the longer the opener, the less it reads as real.
- The AI-slop opener is described as the number one giveaway of an AI-written
  cold email at time of recording — it names something no human would actually notice.
- Do not put pricing in a cold email.
- Do not use ranges in an offer; the range destroys the specificity that makes it credible.
- Do not say "we" when you can say "I".
- "When you know the rules you can break them" — said about longer, chattier
  personalization working for some senders; flagged as not for beginners.
- Stated preference ≠ revealed preference. Optimize against customer behavior,
  not what customers say they want.
- Intuition about which copy will win is unreliable; the market is the only proxy.
