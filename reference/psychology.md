# Why strangers say yes — the seven principles

Source: the course's opening module, presenter credits Robert Cialdini's
*Influence* as the organizing source. CONFIRMED by both readings.

These are the *why* under the four-part structure. You don't cite them in copy —
you use them to diagnose why a draft isn't landing.

## 1. Give first
Provide something valuable before asking. Creates obligation, lowers resistance.
The restaurant breath mints; the Costco sample. In outbound: give an insight
without asking for anything. "Noticed X is misconfigured on your landing page,
here's how to fix it." The ask is inferred without being made.

## 2. Micro commitments
Escalate in small steps. Each yes makes the next yes easier. Never ask for the
big thing first. This is why the CTA is a 15-minute call, not a proposal.

## 3. Social proof
Others like them already trust you. This is what Part 2 of the email does.

## 4. Authority
Credentials, named clients, numbers. Borrowed credibility counts — working with
a known name transfers some of their standing to you.

## 5. Rapport
The sense that you're a real person having a conversation. Carried by the frame
rules: casual, "I" not "we", slightly imperfect.

## 6. Scarcity
Limited time, limited slots. Use sparingly in cold — it reads as a sales tactic
if it isn't true.

## 7. Shared identity
The presenter calls this the most important of the seven. You are in their
in-group. This is why Part 2 picks a client *like them*, and why cold reading
works — "very no BS" says you value what they value.

## Diagnosing with these

If a draft feels off, name which principle it's missing:
- Reads transactional → no give-first, no rapport
- Reads like a pitch to anyone → no shared identity
- Big ask, no reply → skipped the micro commitment
- "Why should I believe you?" → no social proof or authority

## The honest note

The presenter's own framing: this is documented psychology, not a trick, and you
are already on the receiving end of it every day. That said — everything here
works because it's *true*. Cold reading that names something real about the
prospect builds trust. Cold reading that fabricates a relationship is a lie that
gets found out on the call. Use the principles to be relevant, not to deceive.
